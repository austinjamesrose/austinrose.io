## The Mandate

I joined Airtable as a People Technology Architect to build the systems the People team runs on. The job was infrastructure, the kind people and increasingly AI agents depend on every day.

My first weeks were breadth. Discovery interviews, workshops, agent experiments, office hours, and production fixes across half a dozen People systems. The gap was obvious. There was no single governed source of truth for workforce data, and no safe way for the AI tools everyone was excited about to consume it. So I made the case for one and built it.

## The People Data Warehouse

I architected the company's People Data Warehouse on Databricks and shipped the initial production release in five weeks. It has a certified layer of workforce and recruiting data models, a canonical registry of 99 metrics, and a data-discovery layer designed for both analysts and AI agents.

The goal was a warehouse people could trust: one definition per metric, reconciled against source systems on every quantitative surface, with a clear path from raw data to certified.

## Governance and Privacy, From Day One

I built data privacy and security controls in during the first week:

- Row-level access control (RBAC)
- Automated masking of protected-category and PII fields
- Column-level sensitivity classification across the warehouse
- Small-cell suppression so aggregates can't re-identify individuals
- Credential-free deployment pipelines (OIDC), so no static credentials live in the repo

The result is a warehouse that is audit-ready by design. Security tooling (secret scanning, static analysis, and an adversarial review gate) runs in CI on every change.

## Built for AI Agents

The warehouse is now in production with agent-first consumption. Instead of waiting on a report, someone on the People team asks an AI agent a specific question, a slice of hiring history or a mobility trend, and gets a trustworthy answer in seconds, drawn from certified data.

That works because I built the data-discovery layer for machines and for people: a machine-readable data dictionary and object catalog, explicit signals for what is directly consumable, and a daily harness that scores how well an agent can find and navigate the data. Governed shares feed other functions, and additional data domains are onboarding now.

## Beyond the Warehouse

The warehouse anchored a broader People Technology practice:

- **AI governance program** for the People org: an intake registry, a review desk, and a certification workflow for org-facing agent builds.
- **Quarterly check-in, redesigned:** I led the redesign of the company's quarterly check-in as an agent-driven reflection tool, launched company-wide for the August '26 cycle.
- **AI enablement:** I led AI enablement and change management for the People org, with recurring office hours, build jams, and workshops for recruiters, executive assistants, and company-wide onboarding.

## How It Got Built

I was the sole engineer on the build, orchestrating parallel AI coding agents behind mandatory adversarial review gates, which compressed a multi-engineer, multi-quarter scope into a single-engineer project. Orchestration threads acted as technical lead, coordinating agents scoped to single tasks, with dedicated review sessions before anything merged and a blind agent that used the warehouse as an analyst to catch what I would miss. The engineering practices (branch conventions, in-repo doctrine, agent instructions) were written so any coding agent could follow them, and the warehouse ran on exactly those practices.
